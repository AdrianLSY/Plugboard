## Purpose

Defines how a sidecar proves it is entitled to serve a particular mount for a particular tenant, and how the credentials that carry that proof are issued, protected at rest, presented, rotated, revoked, audited, and reaped. Authentication is the gate in front of every tunnel, so its cost, its failure behaviour, and the promptness of revocation are tenant-isolation properties rather than convenience features.

## ADDED Requirements

### Requirement: A credential is scoped to one mount and one tenant

A sidecar credential SHALL be bound at issuance to exactly one mount and to the tenant that owns it. Presenting the credential SHALL authorise serving that mount and nothing else. The scope SHALL be fixed at issuance and SHALL NOT be widened afterwards by any operation short of issuing a new credential.

A credential SHALL be issuable only against a mount that exists, is not deleted, and is a valid mount target. The issued record SHALL carry an expiry instant chosen at issuance, an optional operator-supplied label, and the identity of the principal that issued it.

A credential SHALL NOT be issued without an expiry instant, and SHALL NOT be issued with a lifetime exceeding a configured maximum for sidecar credentials. A request that omits an expiry or exceeds that maximum SHALL be refused rather than silently adjusted.

#### Scenario: Credential serves only its own mount

- **WHEN** a sidecar presents a credential issued for one mount and asks to serve a different mount
- **THEN** the tunnel is not authorised for the second mount
- **AND** no traffic for the second mount is routed to that sidecar

#### Scenario: Credential does not cross a tenant boundary

- **WHEN** a credential issued for a mount owned by one tenant is presented against a mount owned by another tenant
- **THEN** authentication fails
- **AND** the outcome is identical to presenting a credential that does not exist

#### Scenario: Issuance against an ineligible target is refused

- **WHEN** a principal requests a credential for a path that is not a valid mount target, or for a mount that has been deleted
- **THEN** no credential is created
- **AND** the caller receives an error naming the ineligibility, distinct from an authorisation failure

#### Scenario: Scope cannot be widened after issuance

- **WHEN** an operator submits a change to an existing credential's mount, tenant, or expiry instant
- **THEN** the change is refused
- **AND** the stored mount, tenant, and expiry instant are unchanged
- **AND** a change to the label alone succeeds

#### Scenario: Unbounded lifetime is refused

- **WHEN** a principal requests a credential carrying no expiry instant
- **AND** another principal requests one whose lifetime exceeds the configured maximum for sidecar credentials
- **THEN** neither credential is created
- **AND** each caller receives an error stating that an expiry within the configured maximum is required

### Requirement: The system generates the secret and the caller cannot influence it

The secret value of a credential SHALL be generated by the system at issuance from a cryptographically strong source, with at least a configured minimum entropy. A caller SHALL NOT be able to supply, seed, or otherwise influence the secret, and no two issuances SHALL produce the same secret.

The presentation SHALL contain a non-secret component sufficient to resolve it to at most one stored record, and that component alone SHALL NOT authenticate. The secret SHALL NOT be derivable from the credential identifier, the mount, the tenant, the label, or the issuance time.

#### Scenario: Caller-supplied secret is not honoured

- **WHEN** a principal requests a credential and supplies a secret value of its own choosing
- **THEN** the credential is either refused or created with a system-generated secret
- **AND** the supplied value does not authenticate

#### Scenario: Secrets are distinct across issuances

- **WHEN** many credentials are issued for the same mount by the same principal
- **THEN** no two of them carry the same secret value

#### Scenario: The identifier alone does not authenticate

- **WHEN** the non-secret component of a valid credential is presented without its secret
- **THEN** authentication fails
- **AND** the outcome is the same indistinguishable authentication failure

### Requirement: Every credential operation is authorised against an acting principal

Issuing, listing, reading, relabelling, revoking, and reaping a credential SHALL each require an acting principal, and SHALL be authorised against that principal's authority over the mount the credential is scoped to. No credential operation SHALL be reachable without an acting principal.

A principal without sufficient authority over the mount SHALL be unable to observe whether a credential for that mount exists.

#### Scenario: Insufficient authority cannot issue

- **WHEN** a principal with read-only authority over a mount requests a credential for it
- **THEN** no credential is created
- **AND** the caller receives an authorisation failure

#### Scenario: Mutation cannot cross a tenant boundary

- **WHEN** a principal attempts to relabel or revoke a credential scoped to a mount they have no authority over
- **THEN** the operation has no effect on stored state
- **AND** the credential continues to authenticate

#### Scenario: Existence is not disclosed to an unauthorised principal

- **WHEN** an unauthorised principal requests a credential record by its identifier
- **THEN** the response is the same whether or not a credential with that identifier exists

### Requirement: The secret is presented exactly once

The secret value of a credential SHALL be returned to the caller only in the result of the operation that creates it. No subsequent read, list, export, diagnostic, or administrative operation SHALL return it. The creating operation SHALL make clear that the value cannot be retrieved again.

No operation SHALL re-display, re-derive, or regenerate the secret of an existing credential in place. A lost secret SHALL be remediable only by issuing a replacement credential, which SHALL be a distinct record with a distinct identifier.

#### Scenario: Creation returns the secret

- **WHEN** a credential is created
- **THEN** the result contains the secret value
- **AND** it contains the credential's identifier, mount, expiry instant, and label

#### Scenario: Subsequent reads never return the secret

- **WHEN** the same credential is read or listed after creation
- **THEN** the result contains the identifier, mount, expiry instant, label, and status
- **AND** it contains no field from which the secret can be reconstructed

#### Scenario: No operation regenerates a secret in place

- **WHEN** a principal invokes every operation the system exposes for an existing credential
- **THEN** none of them returns a secret value
- **AND** the credential's stored protection state is unchanged by any of them

#### Scenario: A replacement is a distinct record

- **WHEN** a replacement credential is issued for a mount whose secret was lost
- **THEN** the replacement carries a different identifier and a different secret
- **AND** the original credential can be revoked without affecting the replacement

### Requirement: Stored credential state cannot be turned back into a credential

Stored credential state SHALL NOT permit recovery of any secret value, and SHALL NOT be presentable as a credential. An adversary holding a complete copy of the credential store, and of every backup and replica of it, SHALL NOT thereby be able to authenticate as any sidecar.

Two credential records protecting the same secret value SHALL NOT produce identical stored state, so the store does not reveal that two records share a secret.

The parameters governing this protection SHALL be identical in every environment in which the system runs. No environment, configuration profile, or test harness SHALL weaken or disable them.

#### Scenario: The store yields no usable credential

- **WHEN** the entire contents of the credential store are read
- **AND** every stored field is presented verbatim as a credential
- **THEN** authentication fails for every one of them

#### Scenario: Identical secrets do not collide in storage

- **WHEN** two credential records are created protecting the same secret value
- **THEN** their stored representations differ
- **AND** neither can be identified as matching the other by comparing stored state

#### Scenario: Protection parameters are the same in every environment

- **WHEN** the protection parameters in effect are read in a non-production environment
- **THEN** they are identical to the parameters in effect in production

#### Scenario: Weakened protection fails the build

- **WHEN** the protection parameters are reduced below their configured minimum in any environment
- **THEN** the property assertion fails
- **AND** the failure names the parameter that was weakened

### Requirement: A sidecar protects its own stored credential at rest

A sidecar that persists a credential between restarts SHALL store it under authenticated encryption, keyed from material supplied by the operator of that sidecar rather than from a value embedded in the sidecar or derivable from the stored record itself. Each stored record SHALL be encrypted independently, so that a record copied out of one sidecar's store is not usable in another's.

Decryption with the wrong key material, or of a record whose stored bytes have been altered, SHALL fail closed: the sidecar SHALL refuse to use the record, SHALL report the failure distinguishably from having no credential at all, and SHALL NOT proceed with a partially decrypted or unauthenticated value. The sidecar SHALL NOT write the credential in recoverable form to any location other than that store.

#### Scenario: Wrong key material yields nothing

- **WHEN** a sidecar is started with key material other than the material its stored credential was encrypted under
- **THEN** it does not obtain a credential from the store
- **AND** it does not attempt to establish a tunnel with a value derived from the failed decryption
- **AND** the reported failure names an unreadable store, distinct from an empty store

#### Scenario: Tampering with a stored record is detected

- **WHEN** any octet of a stored credential record is altered
- **THEN** the sidecar refuses to use that record
- **AND** it does not present any value derived from it

#### Scenario: A copied record is not portable

- **WHEN** a stored credential record is copied from one sidecar's store into another sidecar's store
- **THEN** the second sidecar does not obtain a usable credential from it

#### Scenario: No plaintext copy is written elsewhere

- **WHEN** a sidecar obtains, stores, uses, and renews a credential
- **THEN** the credential appears in plaintext in no file, log, or emitted diagnostic it produces

### Requirement: Authentication binds to the tunnel, not to individual requests

A credential SHALL be presented when a tunnel is established, and the resulting authenticated identity — credential identifier, mount, and tenant — SHALL be bound to that tunnel for its lifetime. A request carried over an established tunnel SHALL NOT be separately authenticated, and SHALL NOT be able to alter the mount or the tenant that tunnel is authorised for.

Credential material appearing anywhere in proxied client traffic SHALL have no authenticating effect. The proxy SHALL NOT interpret any field, target component, or body content of a proxied client request as a sidecar credential.

#### Scenario: Requests inherit the tunnel's authorisation

- **WHEN** many requests are carried over one authenticated tunnel
- **THEN** each is served under the identity bound at establishment
- **AND** no request causes a further credential to be presented or verified

#### Scenario: A request cannot widen the tunnel's scope

- **WHEN** a request carried over a tunnel asserts a mount or tenant other than the one bound at establishment
- **THEN** the assertion has no effect
- **AND** the request is handled under the bound mount and tenant, or refused

#### Scenario: Client traffic carrying credential-shaped material authenticates nothing

- **WHEN** a client sends a proxied request containing a valid sidecar credential in a header field, in the request target, or in the body
- **THEN** no tunnel, mount, or authorisation is granted on account of it
- **AND** the value is carried to the backend as ordinary request content or removed by edge hygiene, never consumed as a credential

### Requirement: A sidecar verifies what it is about to authenticate to

Authentication in this system runs in one direction only: the sidecar proves its entitlement to the proxy. The sidecar therefore SHALL establish that the endpoint it has connected to is the one it was configured to reach, before it presents a credential. It SHALL verify the endpoint's presented identity against the configured endpoint, SHALL refuse to present a credential where verification fails or where the connection carries no verifiable identity at all, and SHALL report that refusal distinguishably from an authentication failure returned by the proxy.

Verification SHALL NOT be disableable by a value that can be set without being seen: where a deployment permits an endpoint identity the sidecar cannot verify against a public trust path, it SHALL be by explicitly configuring the identity to expect, never by a setting that accepts any identity. A sidecar started in a configuration that would present a credential to an unverified endpoint SHALL refuse to start.

Without this, the credential is the only secret in the system that a sidecar volunteers to whatever answers a hostname, and a redirected or hijacked endpoint obtains a working credential for a mount in the tenant's own infrastructure.

#### Scenario: An unverifiable endpoint receives no credential

- **WHEN** a sidecar connects to an endpoint whose presented identity does not verify against the configured endpoint
- **THEN** no credential is presented
- **AND** no tunnel is established
- **AND** the reported failure is distinguishable from an authentication failure returned by the proxy

#### Scenario: A connection with no verifiable identity receives no credential

- **WHEN** a sidecar's configured endpoint resolves to a connection that carries no verifiable endpoint identity
- **THEN** no credential is presented
- **AND** the condition is reported rather than retried silently

#### Scenario: Verification cannot be switched off blindly

- **WHEN** a sidecar is configured to accept any endpoint identity
- **THEN** it refuses to start
- **AND** the refusal names that setting

#### Scenario: An explicitly pinned identity is honoured

- **WHEN** a sidecar is configured with a specific endpoint identity to expect and connects to an endpoint presenting it
- **THEN** verification succeeds
- **AND** the credential is presented and the tunnel is established

### Requirement: Authentication cost does not grow with installation size

The work performed to authenticate one presented credential SHALL be bounded and SHALL NOT grow with the number of credentials in the installation, the tenant, or the mount. A presentation SHALL be resolved to at most one candidate record by its non-secret component, and no other stored record's protection material SHALL be exercised during the attempt.

Authentication SHALL NOT serialise on a lock, a connection from a shared pool, or a single process shared across concurrent attempts, and SHALL NOT hold any such resource for the duration of the attempt. Exhausting the supply of concurrent authentications SHALL NOT prevent unrelated work from making progress.

#### Scenario: Cost is flat across installation size

- **WHEN** the number of stored credentials in an installation is increased by an order of magnitude
- **THEN** the number of stored records examined to authenticate one presentation is unchanged
- **AND** it remains at most one

#### Scenario: An unresolvable presentation examines no more records than a valid one

- **WHEN** a credential that corresponds to no stored record is presented
- **THEN** at most one stored record is examined
- **AND** no other record's protection material is exercised
- **AND** the count does not change as the installation grows

#### Scenario: Concurrent authentications do not serialise

- **WHEN** many sidecars present credentials simultaneously
- **THEN** the attempts proceed concurrently
- **AND** the elapsed time for all of them together is substantially less than the sum of their individual times
- **AND** no attempt waits on a lock or pool entry held by another attempt

#### Scenario: Authentication does not exhaust a shared pool

- **WHEN** the number of simultaneous authentication attempts exceeds the size of any shared resource pool the system holds
- **THEN** unrelated work that uses that pool continues to make progress

### Requirement: Expiry is enforced at presentation

Authentication SHALL reject a credential whose expiry instant has passed. The rejection SHALL occur before the presented secret is verified against any stored secret-protection material, so that an expired credential cannot be used to compel that work.

The authoritative expiry SHALL be the one recorded in the credential store. An expiry value carried inside the presented credential SHALL NOT be trusted as the sole source, and a presentation whose self-asserted expiry disagrees with the stored record SHALL be rejected.

#### Scenario: Expired credential is rejected

- **WHEN** a credential whose expiry instant has passed is presented
- **THEN** authentication fails
- **AND** no tunnel is established
- **AND** the mount is not served by that sidecar

#### Scenario: Expiry check precedes secret verification

- **WHEN** an expired credential is presented with a correct secret
- **AND** an expired credential is presented with an incorrect secret
- **THEN** both fail
- **AND** neither causes verification of the presented secret against stored protection material

#### Scenario: Self-asserted expiry is not authoritative

- **WHEN** a presentation carries an expiry instant later than the one recorded for that credential
- **AND** the recorded instant has passed
- **THEN** authentication fails

#### Scenario: Expiry during an established tunnel

- **WHEN** a credential expires while a tunnel authenticated by it is established
- **THEN** the tunnel is terminated within a configured bound
- **AND** subsequent authentication attempts with that credential fail

### Requirement: Revocation takes effect for future and established connections

Revoking a credential SHALL cause every subsequent authentication with it to fail, within a configured bound of the revocation being recorded, across every instance of the installation.

Revocation SHALL additionally terminate every tunnel already established with that credential, within the same bound. In-flight exchanges on a terminated tunnel SHALL be failed explicitly, using the contract's enumerated error mechanism, rather than left to time out. The reason delivered to the already-authenticated sidecar SHALL distinguish revocation from a transport failure, so that it does not reconnect with the same credential.

Revoking a credential SHALL NOT prevent issuing a new credential for the same mount, and a revoked record SHALL NOT occupy any uniqueness constraint that a live credential needs.

#### Scenario: Revoked credential cannot authenticate

- **WHEN** a credential is revoked
- **AND** a sidecar presents it
- **THEN** authentication fails
- **AND** the outcome is identical to presenting a credential that does not exist

#### Scenario: Revocation terminates an established tunnel

- **WHEN** a sidecar is serving traffic over a tunnel authenticated by a credential
- **AND** that credential is revoked
- **THEN** the tunnel is terminated within the configured bound
- **AND** the mount is no longer served by that sidecar

#### Scenario: In-flight exchanges fail explicitly

- **WHEN** a tunnel is terminated because its credential was revoked
- **THEN** every exchange in flight on that tunnel is failed with a stated reason
- **AND** no client waits for the exchange to time out

#### Scenario: The sidecar does not reconnect with the revoked credential

- **WHEN** a sidecar's tunnel is terminated for revocation
- **THEN** the reason it receives identifies the credential as no longer valid
- **AND** it is distinguishable from a transport failure or a restart of the proxy

#### Scenario: Revocation propagates across instances

- **WHEN** a credential is revoked on one instance of a multi-instance installation
- **AND** a tunnel authenticated by it is established against a different instance
- **THEN** that tunnel is terminated within the configured bound

#### Scenario: Revocation concurrent with an authentication in progress

- **WHEN** a credential is revoked while an authentication attempt using it is already under way
- **THEN** either the attempt fails, or the tunnel it establishes is terminated within the configured bound
- **AND** no tunnel authenticated by that credential remains established beyond that bound

#### Scenario: A revoked record does not block re-issuance

- **WHEN** every credential for a mount has been revoked
- **AND** a principal with authority issues a new credential for that mount
- **THEN** the new credential is created
- **AND** it authenticates

### Requirement: An unreachable credential store fails authentication closed without severing service

When the durable state that authentication and revocation depend on cannot be reached, the system SHALL refuse every new authentication rather than admit one it cannot check. A tunnel SHALL NOT be established on the strength of a cached, assumed, or previously successful authentication result.

Tunnels already established SHALL continue to serve. The unreachable state SHALL NOT be treated as evidence that a credential has been revoked, that a mount has been deleted, or that a credential has expired, and SHALL NOT by itself terminate any tunnel.

While the state is unreachable the revocation and expiry bounds this capability requires cannot be honoured. The system SHALL therefore surface the condition continuously as a degraded state naming the affected bound and the time the condition began, and SHALL record that revocations recorded during the period may not yet be in force. On recovery the system SHALL re-evaluate the credentials of established tunnels and enforce any revocation or expiry that occurred during the period, within the configured bound measured from recovery.

A refusal caused by the unreachable state SHALL be the same indistinguishable authentication failure the presenter receives for every other cause, so that it discloses nothing; the specific cause SHALL be recorded in the audit trail and in the degraded-state inventory.

#### Scenario: New authentication is refused, not assumed

- **WHEN** the credential store is unreachable and a sidecar presents a valid credential
- **THEN** authentication fails
- **AND** no tunnel is established
- **AND** the presenter receives the same indistinguishable authentication failure it would receive for an unknown credential

#### Scenario: Established tunnels keep serving

- **WHEN** the credential store becomes unreachable while tunnels are established and serving
- **THEN** those tunnels remain established
- **AND** their mounts continue to be served
- **AND** no tunnel is terminated on the ground that the store could not be reached

#### Scenario: The unmeetable bound is surfaced

- **WHEN** the credential store has been unreachable for a period
- **THEN** the condition is observable continuously with its onset time
- **AND** it names the revocation and expiry bounds as not currently enforceable

#### Scenario: Revocation is enforced on recovery

- **WHEN** a credential is revoked while the store is unreachable from an instance holding a tunnel authenticated by it
- **AND** the store becomes reachable again
- **THEN** that tunnel is terminated within the configured bound measured from recovery
- **AND** the termination reason names revocation

#### Scenario: The cause is recorded even though it is not disclosed

- **WHEN** an authentication is refused because the store was unreachable
- **THEN** the audit trail records that specific cause
- **AND** it is distinguishable there from an unknown, expired, or revoked credential

### Requirement: Mount lifecycle bounds credential validity

Deleting a mount SHALL cause every credential scoped to it to stop authenticating, and SHALL terminate tunnels established with those credentials, with the same promptness required of revocation. Restoring or re-creating a mount SHALL NOT restore the authenticating power of credentials issued before its deletion.

Transferring a mount to a different owning principal SHALL NOT leave a previously issued credential able to serve it.

#### Scenario: Mount deletion invalidates its credentials

- **WHEN** a mount is deleted
- **THEN** credentials scoped to it no longer authenticate
- **AND** tunnels established with them are terminated

#### Scenario: Re-creating a mount does not revive old credentials

- **WHEN** a mount is deleted and a mount at the same location is subsequently created
- **THEN** credentials issued before the deletion do not authenticate against the new mount
- **AND** serving the new mount requires newly issued credentials

#### Scenario: Ownership change invalidates prior credentials

- **WHEN** authority over a mount is transferred to a different principal
- **THEN** credentials issued under the prior authority no longer authenticate
- **AND** the change is recorded in the audit trail

### Requirement: Authentication failures are indistinguishable to the presenter

An unknown credential, an expired credential, a revoked credential, a malformed credential, and a credential whose mount no longer exists SHALL all produce the same outcome to the presenter: the same enumerated error, the same accompanying description, and no additional field that varies with the cause.

No failure SHALL confirm that a credential identifier exists, that a mount exists, or that a tenant exists. The failure path SHALL NOT branch on whether the presentation resolved to a stored record in a way observable to the presenter, including through the time taken: an unknown identifier and a known-but-unusable identifier SHALL be indistinguishable in both content and elapsed time.

This requirement constrains only what is disclosed to a party that has not authenticated. A party already authenticated on a tunnel that is subsequently terminated SHALL be told the specific cause, as required elsewhere in this capability, because disclosing it to that party reveals nothing it did not already establish.

#### Scenario: Every rejection looks the same

- **WHEN** an unknown, an expired, a revoked, a malformed, and a mount-deleted credential are each presented
- **THEN** all five presenters receive the same enumerated error and description
- **AND** no field of the response differs between them

#### Scenario: Failure does not confirm existence

- **WHEN** a syntactically well-formed credential for a mount that does not exist is presented
- **THEN** the error and description conveyed are identical to those for a mount that does exist presented with a wrong secret
- **AND** nothing in the response indicates whether the mount, the tenant, or the credential identifier exists

#### Scenario: Timing does not reveal whether the identifier exists

- **WHEN** presentations bearing an identifier that exists but is expired or revoked, and presentations bearing an identifier that has never existed, are each repeated many times
- **THEN** the distribution of elapsed time does not separate the two groups

#### Scenario: Authentication failure carries its own enumerated code

- **WHEN** authentication fails for any cause
- **THEN** the enumerated error conveyed identifies an authentication failure and no narrower cause
- **AND** it is a different enumerated code from those conveyed for a transport failure and for a refusal to establish the tunnel for any non-authentication reason

### Requirement: The audit trail distinguishes what the presenter cannot

Every authentication attempt, successful or not, SHALL be recorded with its specific cause, the mount and tenant it was directed at, the credential identifier where one was resolved, the observed source of the attempt, and the time. Every issuance, relabelling, rotation, and revocation SHALL be recorded with the acting principal.

These records SHALL be written to the one audit trail the tenancy capability defines, and SHALL inherit its properties — append-only, immutable once written, removable only by an operator, and readable only within the scope of the reading principal's grants. This capability SHALL NOT maintain a second trail.

Audit records SHALL NOT contain the presented secret or any value from which it could be derived. An operator with authority over a tenant SHALL be able to retrieve the audit records for that tenant and no others.

#### Scenario: Operator can tell the causes apart

- **WHEN** an unknown, an expired, and a revoked credential are each presented and each rejected identically
- **THEN** the audit trail records three attempts with three distinct causes

#### Scenario: Audit carries no secret material

- **WHEN** every audit record produced by an authentication attempt is read
- **THEN** none of them contains the presented secret
- **AND** none contains stored protection material

#### Scenario: Audit is tenant-scoped

- **WHEN** an operator with authority over one tenant queries the audit trail
- **THEN** they see records for that tenant's mounts
- **AND** they see no record belonging to another tenant

#### Scenario: Administrative actions are attributed

- **WHEN** a credential is issued and later revoked by different principals
- **THEN** the audit trail names the issuing principal and the revoking principal separately

### Requirement: Rotation replaces a credential without interrupting service

A sidecar SHALL be able to obtain a replacement credential for the same mount while its current credential is still valid, and SHALL be able to begin using the replacement without dropping its tunnel or failing any exchange in flight.

The previous credential SHALL remain valid until its own expiry or until it is explicitly revoked, so that both are usable during the changeover. A sidecar performing rotation SHALL NOT be required to re-establish its tunnel, and SHALL NOT lose its position in mount selection.

#### Scenario: Rotation drops no exchange

- **WHEN** a sidecar rotates its credential while continuously serving traffic
- **THEN** every request in flight at the moment of the change completes
- **AND** no client observes an error attributable to the rotation

#### Scenario: Both credentials are valid during the overlap

- **WHEN** a replacement credential has been issued and the previous one has not yet expired
- **THEN** either credential authenticates a new tunnel for that mount
- **AND** the audit trail records which one was used

#### Scenario: Rotation does not require reconnection

- **WHEN** a sidecar rotates its credential
- **THEN** its existing tunnel remains established
- **AND** it continues to receive traffic for the mount throughout

#### Scenario: Replacement is refused when the old credential is invalid

- **WHEN** a sidecar attempts to obtain a replacement using an expired or revoked credential
- **THEN** no replacement is issued
- **AND** the failure is the same indistinguishable authentication failure

#### Scenario: Rotation cannot change scope

- **WHEN** a sidecar requests a replacement credential for a mount other than the one its current credential is scoped to
- **THEN** no replacement is issued
- **AND** the attempt is recorded in the audit trail

#### Scenario: Rotation cannot extend beyond the maximum lifetime

- **WHEN** a sidecar rotates repeatedly over a long period
- **THEN** no credential it holds at any point has a lifetime exceeding the configured maximum
- **AND** rotation does not extend the expiry of the credential it already holds

### Requirement: Rotation attempts are spread across a fleet

A sidecar SHALL attempt renewal at a point that leaves a margin sufficient for repeated retries before its credential expires. The chosen point SHALL be randomised within a bound, independently per sidecar, so that sidecars sharing a start time or a credential lifetime do not converge on the same instant.

Retries after a failed renewal SHALL be spaced with an increasing interval that is also randomised, and the interval SHALL NOT collapse back to its smallest value as the number of attempts grows.

#### Scenario: A simultaneously started fleet does not synchronise

- **WHEN** many sidecars are started at the same instant with credentials of the same lifetime
- **THEN** their first renewal attempts are spread across the randomisation interval
- **AND** the span between the earliest and the latest attempt is a substantial proportion of that interval
- **AND** no two sidecars derive their offset from a value shared across the fleet

#### Scenario: Renewal leaves room to retry

- **WHEN** a sidecar's first renewal attempt fails
- **THEN** it has time to make further attempts before its credential expires
- **AND** it continues serving traffic in the meantime

#### Scenario: Backoff does not wrap around

- **WHEN** renewal fails continuously for a very large number of attempts
- **THEN** the interval between attempts remains at or below its configured maximum
- **AND** it never returns to the initial interval

### Requirement: Renewal failure is surfaced before it becomes an outage

A sidecar that has failed to renew SHALL make the condition observable while its current credential is still valid, and the signal SHALL carry the time remaining before expiry. The signal SHALL escalate as the remaining time shrinks.

The proxy SHALL make the impending expiry of a credential for a served mount observable to the operator of that tenant, independently of whether the sidecar reports it. A mount whose only connected sidecar holds a credential about to expire SHALL be identifiable before the expiry occurs.

#### Scenario: Sidecar reports failing renewal

- **WHEN** a sidecar's renewal attempts have been failing and its credential is still valid
- **THEN** its readiness signal reflects the degraded state
- **AND** the reported state includes the time remaining before expiry

#### Scenario: Escalation as expiry approaches

- **WHEN** renewal continues to fail and the remaining lifetime falls below a configured threshold
- **THEN** the severity of the signal increases
- **AND** the change is observable without inspecting the sidecar's internals

#### Scenario: Operator sees the impending expiry from the proxy side

- **WHEN** a mount is served only by sidecars whose credentials expire within a configured window
- **THEN** the mount is identifiable to the tenant's operator as at risk
- **AND** the identification does not depend on the sidecar reporting anything

#### Scenario: Expiry after unreported failure is still attributable

- **WHEN** a credential expires and its tunnel is terminated
- **THEN** the audit trail records the termination as caused by expiry
- **AND** it is distinguishable from revocation and from a transport failure

### Requirement: Automated issuance uses a distinct, bounded credential class

The system SHALL provide a second class of credential whose only power is to mint short-lived sidecar credentials within a scope fixed at its own issuance. This class SHALL be distinguishable from a sidecar credential at every point it is handled, and SHALL NOT itself authenticate a tunnel or serve a mount.

An issuing credential SHALL carry its own expiry, and SHALL be subject to a maximum lifetime bound configured separately from and independently of the bound on sidecar credentials. A mint request whose requested scope exceeds the issuing credential's scope, or whose requested expiry is later than the issuing credential's own expiry, SHALL be refused rather than clamped. An issuing credential SHALL NOT mint another issuing credential, nor widen its own scope.

Issuing credentials SHALL be subject to the same at-rest protection, one-time presentation, indistinguishable failure, revocation promptness, use tracking, and audit requirements as sidecar credentials.

#### Scenario: Issuing credential cannot serve a mount

- **WHEN** an issuing credential is presented in order to establish a tunnel
- **THEN** authentication fails
- **AND** the outcome is the same indistinguishable authentication failure

#### Scenario: Minted credential inherits and cannot exceed the scope

- **WHEN** an issuing credential scoped to one mount is used to mint a sidecar credential
- **THEN** the minted credential is scoped to that mount and its tenant
- **AND** a request to mint for any other mount is refused

#### Scenario: Minting beyond the issuer's expiry is refused

- **WHEN** a mint request asks for a sidecar credential whose expiry is later than the issuing credential's own expiry
- **THEN** no credential is created
- **AND** the caller receives an error naming the issuer's expiry as the limit

#### Scenario: An expired issuer cannot mint

- **WHEN** an issuing credential's expiry has passed and a mint request is made with it
- **THEN** no credential is created
- **AND** the failure is the same indistinguishable authentication failure
- **AND** sidecar credentials it minted before expiring continue to authenticate until their own expiry or revocation

#### Scenario: Privilege cannot be escalated

- **WHEN** an issuing credential is used to request another issuing credential, or to request a scope wider than its own
- **THEN** the request is refused
- **AND** the attempt is recorded in the audit trail

#### Scenario: Issuing credentials are separately bounded

- **WHEN** an issuing credential is requested with a lifetime exceeding the configured maximum for its class
- **THEN** no issuing credential is created
- **AND** the error states the maximum that was exceeded
- **AND** the bound applied is the one for issuing credentials, not the one for sidecar credentials

### Requirement: Minted credentials are attributable to their issuer

Every credential produced by an issuing credential SHALL record the identity of the issuing credential that produced it. An operator SHALL be able to enumerate every credential minted by a given issuing credential, and to revoke them as a set in one operation.

Revoking an issuing credential SHALL immediately stop further minting with it. Whether credentials it already minted are revoked with it SHALL be a stated choice at the point of revocation, and the choice made SHALL be recorded.

#### Scenario: Minted set is enumerable

- **WHEN** an issuing credential has minted several sidecar credentials
- **THEN** an operator can list exactly those credentials
- **AND** each names the issuing credential that produced it

#### Scenario: Revoking the issuer stops minting

- **WHEN** an issuing credential is revoked
- **AND** a further mint request is made with it
- **THEN** the request fails with the indistinguishable authentication failure
- **AND** no credential is created

#### Scenario: Cascading revocation is explicit

- **WHEN** an operator revokes an issuing credential
- **THEN** they state whether credentials already minted by it are revoked as well
- **AND** the choice and its effect are recorded in the audit trail

### Requirement: Every credential-accepting endpoint is rate limited on an unforgeable attribute

Every endpoint that accepts a credential — tunnel establishment, rotation, and minting alike — SHALL be rate limited, and the limiting key SHALL be derived from an attribute the caller cannot choose. It SHALL NOT be derived from any value supplied in the request that the system has not itself authenticated.

Where a presentation **authenticated**, the limit SHALL be keyed on that credential's identity, so that a holder cannot establish, rotate, or mint without bound. Where it did not authenticate — for any cause, including a credential that is unknown, malformed, expired, revoked, or whose non-secret component resolved to a stored record but whose secret did not verify — the limit SHALL be keyed on the observed source of the connection, as the tunnel-listener capability determines that source.

A failed presentation SHALL NOT consume, exhaust, or otherwise affect the allowance of the credential whose non-secret component it bore. The non-secret component is not a secret, so keying failures on it would let any party who learns it exhaust the allowance of the legitimate holder and take that tenant's mount out of service; that is the denial the listener capability forbids, and this requirement is the reason it cannot arise. Reaching a credential's own allowance SHALL refuse further attempts for it without invalidating or revoking it, SHALL NOT extend to any other credential of the same tenant or mount, and SHALL be restored by the passage of time alone.

The scope each of those two keys is counted over SHALL be stated, because the proxy is a multi-instance installation and a limit counted at one instance is not the limit an operator configured. A credential's own allowance, which is reached only after the presentation authenticated and is therefore attributable to a tenant, SHALL be counted across the installation: a holder SHALL NOT obtain a further allowance by directing presentations at another instance, and adding instances SHALL NOT enlarge any credential's allowance. The allowance keyed on the observed source of a presentation that did not authenticate SHALL be accounted per accepting instance, under the closed exception the tenancy capability states and for the reason the listener capability gives, SHALL be declared and observable as per-instance, and SHALL NOT be attributed to any tenant.

Where an instance cannot account a credential's allowance across the installation, it SHALL apply the discipline the installation declares for that condition rather than admitting a fresh allowance locally, and a refusal on that ground SHALL be distinguishable to an operator from a refusal caused by the credential reaching its own allowance. It SHALL NOT be distinguishable to the presenter, which the indistinguishability requirements of this capability already forbid.

Exceeding the limit SHALL NOT reveal whether the presented credential was valid.

#### Scenario: A caller-supplied field cannot mint a fresh allowance

- **WHEN** a caller sends a distinct value in every request for any field it controls
- **THEN** all of those requests share one limit
- **AND** the limit is reached at the same point as for a caller sending a constant value

#### Scenario: Unresolved presentations are limited

- **WHEN** a caller presents a stream of credentials that resolve to nothing
- **THEN** the attempts are limited on an observed property of the connection
- **AND** the rate at which protection work is performed is bounded

#### Scenario: Limits are per credential once it has authenticated

- **WHEN** two different credentials each authenticate repeatedly and one exhausts its allowance
- **THEN** the other continues to be served
- **AND** the exhausted one is refused
- **AND** the refusal does not extend to any other credential of the same tenant or mount

#### Scenario: Wrong secrets against a known identifier do not lock its holder out

- **WHEN** a party presents a credential's non-secret component with an incorrect secret a great many times
- **THEN** those attempts are limited on that party's observed source
- **AND** the legitimate holder of that credential still establishes a tunnel
- **AND** the credential's own allowance is unaffected and the credential is neither invalidated nor revoked

#### Scenario: A credential's allowance is not multiplied across instances

- **WHEN** a holder presents one credential at several instances until its allowance is exhausted in total
- **THEN** a further presentation of it is refused at whichever instance it reaches
- **AND** adding further instances does not admit more presentations of it

#### Scenario: The unauthenticated allowance is declared per instance

- **WHEN** an operator retrieves the limits in force at a credential-accepting endpoint
- **THEN** the allowance keyed on the observed source of an unauthenticated presentation is identified as accounted per accepting instance
- **AND** its consumption is retrievable per instance and is attributed to no tenant
- **AND** the allowance keyed on an authenticated credential's identity is not identified as per-instance

#### Scenario: Degraded accounting does not grant a fresh allowance

- **WHEN** an instance cannot account a credential's allowance across the installation
- **THEN** it does not admit a fresh allowance for that credential on the ground that it holds no local record of prior presentations
- **AND** the refusal is distinguishable to an operator from the credential having reached its own allowance
- **AND** the presenter cannot distinguish the two

#### Scenario: An exhausted allowance is restored without intervention

- **WHEN** a credential exhausts its own allowance and then presents nothing for a period
- **THEN** a subsequent presentation of it is admitted
- **AND** no operator action was required

#### Scenario: Limiting does not leak validity

- **WHEN** the limit is exceeded by a caller presenting a valid credential and by a caller presenting an invalid one
- **THEN** both receive the same outcome

#### Scenario: A flood of tunnel-establishment attempts is bounded

- **WHEN** a caller opens tunnel-establishment attempts as fast as it can with credentials that never resolve
- **THEN** the attempts are refused once the limit for that connection property is reached
- **AND** the rate at which secret-protection work is performed does not rise with the attempt rate
- **AND** tunnels for other sidecars continue to be established throughout

### Requirement: Last use is recorded for every credential

The system SHALL record the time a credential was last successfully used, and SHALL distinguish a credential that has never been used from one used at some past time. The record SHALL survive a restart of the installation, and SHALL be no staler than a configured bound.

Recording use SHALL NOT be on the path that determines the outcome of an authentication: a failure to record SHALL NOT fail an otherwise valid authentication, and the recording SHALL NOT make authentication cost depend on installation size.

An operator SHALL be able to list, for a tenant, credentials that have never been used and credentials unused for longer than a stated period.

#### Scenario: First use is recorded

- **WHEN** a newly issued credential authenticates for the first time
- **THEN** its last-use time is set
- **AND** it is no longer reported as never used

#### Scenario: Last use survives a restart

- **WHEN** a credential is used and the installation is restarted
- **THEN** the recorded last-use time is still present

#### Scenario: Operator can find unused credentials

- **WHEN** an operator queries for credentials unused for longer than a stated period
- **THEN** the result includes credentials whose last use predates that period
- **AND** it includes credentials that have never been used, identified as such

#### Scenario: Recording failure does not fail authentication

- **WHEN** recording the last-use time fails
- **THEN** the authentication still succeeds
- **AND** the recording failure is observable to an operator

### Requirement: Protection material is purpose-separated and versioned

The material used to protect one class of secret SHALL be derived such that it cannot be used against another class, and such that compromise of the material for one purpose does not compromise any other purpose or the root from which it was derived.

Each purpose's material SHALL carry a version, and a stored record SHALL identify the version under which it was protected. More than one version SHALL be servable simultaneously, so that material can be rotated without invalidating every credential at once. A record protected under a version that has been retired SHALL fail authentication as an ordinary, indistinguishable authentication failure rather than as an error.

#### Scenario: Material for one purpose does not work against another

- **WHEN** the material derived for one class of secret is applied to a record of another class
- **THEN** it does not validate that record

#### Scenario: Two versions serve at once

- **WHEN** protection material is rotated to a new version while credentials protected under the prior version are still valid
- **THEN** credentials of both versions authenticate
- **AND** newly issued credentials are protected under the new version

#### Scenario: Retired version fails cleanly

- **WHEN** a prior version is retired and a credential protected under it is presented
- **THEN** authentication fails with the indistinguishable authentication failure
- **AND** the audit trail records the retired version as the cause

#### Scenario: Compromise of one purpose is contained

- **WHEN** the material for one purpose is disclosed
- **THEN** it does not permit deriving the material for any other purpose
- **AND** it does not permit deriving the root material

### Requirement: The installation refuses to start with unsafe credential protection

Before serving any traffic or accepting any tunnel, the installation SHALL validate the configuration from which credential-protection material is derived, and SHALL refuse to start if it is absent, shorter than the configured minimum, or equal to a value published as a development or example default.

Every such failure present SHALL be reported in one refusal, naming each item and what was wrong with it, rather than one item per restart. The refusal SHALL occur before any component that could accept a connection is started.

#### Scenario: Missing material prevents start

- **WHEN** the configuration from which protection material is derived is absent
- **THEN** the installation refuses to start
- **AND** no connection is accepted at any point

#### Scenario: A published default is rejected

- **WHEN** the configured value equals a value published in the project's own example or development configuration
- **THEN** the installation refuses to start
- **AND** the refusal names that specific cause

#### Scenario: All problems are reported at once

- **WHEN** several configuration items required for credential protection are missing or invalid
- **THEN** the refusal names every one of them
- **AND** a subsequent start with one of them fixed names the ones still outstanding

#### Scenario: Undersized material is rejected with its size

- **WHEN** the configured value is shorter than the configured minimum
- **THEN** the installation refuses to start
- **AND** the refusal states the required minimum and the size found

### Requirement: Expired and revoked records are reaped

Credential records that are expired or revoked SHALL be removed from the credential store once a configured retention period has passed, so that stored state does not grow without bound as credentials are rotated.

Reaping SHALL be bounded in the work it performs in any one pass, and SHALL NOT block authentication or issuance while it runs. Audit records SHALL survive the reaping of the credential they describe, under their own retention policy, and SHALL remain attributable to the credential identifier after the credential record is gone.

Reaping SHALL NOT remove a credential that is still capable of authenticating.

#### Scenario: Storage does not grow without bound under rotation

- **WHEN** a mount's credential is rotated continuously for longer than the retention period
- **THEN** the number of stored credential records for that mount remains bounded
- **AND** the current credential is among them

#### Scenario: Reaping does not remove a valid credential

- **WHEN** a reaping pass runs
- **THEN** every credential that has not expired and has not been revoked still authenticates afterwards

#### Scenario: Audit outlives the credential

- **WHEN** a credential record is reaped
- **THEN** audit records naming that credential identifier remain retrievable
- **AND** they still identify the mount, tenant, and issuing principal

#### Scenario: Reaping does not block the authentication path

- **WHEN** a reaping pass runs against a large number of eligible records
- **THEN** authentication and issuance continue to complete throughout
- **AND** the pass is bounded in the work it performs before yielding

### Requirement: Secrets do not appear outside the presentation path

A credential secret SHALL NOT appear in any log line, metric label, telemetry payload, error message, diagnostic dump, stored session or connection state, or request target. Stored protection material SHALL likewise not appear in any of them.

State retained for an authenticated tunnel SHALL carry only what is needed to serve and to revoke: the credential identifier, the mount, and the tenant. It SHALL NOT retain the presented secret or the stored protection material.

#### Scenario: Secrets are absent from logs and diagnostics

- **WHEN** a tunnel is established, serves traffic, and is terminated
- **THEN** no log line, metric, or diagnostic emitted during that time contains the presented secret

#### Scenario: Secrets are absent from request targets

- **WHEN** a sidecar presents a credential to establish a tunnel
- **THEN** no target recorded, logged, or emitted for that attempt contains the secret

#### Scenario: Retained connection state is minimal

- **WHEN** the state retained for an authenticated tunnel is inspected
- **THEN** it contains the credential identifier, the mount, and the tenant
- **AND** it contains neither the presented secret nor stored protection material

#### Scenario: Failure messages carry no secret

- **WHEN** authentication fails for any cause
- **THEN** the error conveyed to the presenter contains no part of the presented value
- **AND** it contains no host-language rendering of an internal value
