## Purpose

Defines where every piece of cryptographic material in the installation lives, what protects it while it is at rest, which classes of it a component is permitted to hold at all, how it reaches every instance that needs it without reaching any instance that does not, and how each class of it is replaced and destroyed. This is the capability the tenant-isolation promise rests on: `routing/custom-domains` requires that a tenant's private key never terminate another tenant's connection and never be readable through any interface, and requires certificate state to be shared across instances; `tunnel/listener` requires its endpoint identity to be replaceable without invalidating a fleet of sidecars pinned to it; and `auth/sidecar-credentials` covers the credentials themselves and explicitly excludes the key material underneath them. Those capabilities own selection, issuance, presentation, and credential lifecycle, and are taken as given here. Material that nobody enumerated is material that nobody rotates, so the inventory is a requirement rather than a document.

## ADDED Requirements

### Requirement: The inventory of custody-managed material is enumerated and closed

The installation SHALL maintain an enumerated inventory of every class of cryptographic material it holds. The inventory SHALL include, at minimum: the private keys of certificates presented for tenant hostnames, the private keys of certificates the platform presents on its own behalf, the endpoint identity material the tunnel listener presents at each of its accept paths — the one a sidecar dials and the internal one a client-facing terminator dials — the material that authenticates a client-facing terminator to the platform, the material that mutually authenticates and protects the cross-instance dispatch hop, the material from which credential-protection material is derived, the material used to protect any other secret held in durable state, the material used to authenticate distribution of material between instances, and any key used to sign or verify a value the platform itself issues.

Each inventory entry SHALL state the class, the purpose the material serves, which principal is entitled to cause it to be replaced, whether it is required for the installation to start, whether a holder is entitled to obtain the material itself or only to have operations performed with it, and the set of holders entitled to either. Where another capability constrains the replacement of a class it owns the use of — an overlap a pinned peer depends on, or a presentation another capability selects — the entry SHALL name that constraint, and the replacement machinery defined here SHALL honour it.

Material that is not an inventory entry SHALL NOT be created, stored, distributed, or used; an attempt to protect a secret with material of an unenumerated class SHALL fail rather than succeed silently.

#### Scenario: Every protected value in durable state names an enumerated class

- **WHEN** every protected value the installation holds in durable state is examined
- **THEN** each names a class present in the inventory
- **AND** a value naming a class absent from the inventory is not recovered or used

#### Scenario: Each entry states its authority, its holders, and whether it can be obtained

- **WHEN** an operator enumerates the custody inventory
- **THEN** each entry states its purpose, the principal entitled to replace it, whether it is required at start, whether a holder can obtain it or only have operations performed with it, and its holder set
- **AND** no entry omits any of those

#### Scenario: Unenumerated material cannot be used

- **WHEN** a secret is presented for protection under material of a class that is not in the inventory
- **THEN** the operation fails
- **AND** no protected record is written
- **AND** the failure is reported to an operator rather than only to the caller

#### Scenario: A class with no rotation authority is a startup failure

- **WHEN** the installation is configured with a class of material whose rotation authority is unstated
- **THEN** the installation refuses to start
- **AND** the refusal names that class

#### Scenario: A class whose replacement another capability constrains names that constraint

- **WHEN** an operator enumerates the entry for the tunnel listener's endpoint identity
- **THEN** the entry names the overlap its replacement must preserve for peers pinned to it
- **AND** a replacement of that class that would not preserve the overlap is refused unless the operator elects it explicitly

#### Scenario: Enumeration carries no material

- **WHEN** an operator enumerates the custody inventory
- **THEN** no entry contains private key material or protection material
- **AND** no entry contains a value from which either could be reconstructed

### Requirement: Key material is generated by the platform from a strong source

Every private key the platform holds SHALL be generated by the platform from a cryptographically strong source at the moment it is needed. No caller, tenant, or request SHALL be able to supply, seed, or otherwise influence the generation, and no key SHALL be derived from a tenant identifier, a hostname, a claim identifier, a creation time, or any other value observable outside the platform.

Each item SHALL receive its own generated material. A key SHALL NOT be shared between two items, SHALL NOT be shared between the platform's own material and any tenant's, and SHALL NOT be reused for a purpose other than the one it was generated for.

Where the source of strong randomness is unavailable or fails, generation SHALL fail closed: no key SHALL be produced from a weaker source, and the operation that required the key SHALL be refused with a distinguishable cause.

#### Scenario: Generation for the same subject twice yields different material

- **WHEN** material is generated twice for the same hostname, in the same tenancy, with every input the platform can observe held identical
- **THEN** the two differ
- **AND** neither is reproducible by an installation configured identically

#### Scenario: No two generated items share material

- **WHEN** material is generated for many items across many tenants
- **THEN** no two items hold the same material
- **AND** no item's material is the material of any other item in the inventory

#### Scenario: A caller cannot influence generation

- **WHEN** a request that causes material to be generated carries a caller-supplied key, seed, or generation parameter
- **THEN** the supplied value is not used
- **AND** the material used is that which the platform generated

#### Scenario: Randomness failure refuses rather than degrades

- **WHEN** the source of strong randomness is unavailable at the moment a key is required
- **THEN** no key is generated
- **AND** the operation requiring it is refused with a cause naming the unavailable source
- **AND** no key derived from a weaker source exists afterwards

### Requirement: Key parameters come from a configured allowlist and a disallowed parameter fails closed

Every item of material SHALL be generated with an algorithm and strength drawn from a configured set of permitted parameters. Generation outside that set SHALL be refused. Material entering the platform from outside SHALL be refused where its parameters are outside that set, at the point of receipt rather than at first use.

Where a stored item's parameters cease to be permitted, the platform SHALL raise an operator-visible condition naming the affected items and SHALL treat them as due for replacement. It SHALL NOT silently continue to present material whose parameters are no longer permitted once a replacement is available, and it SHALL NOT destroy such material before a replacement is in place.

The permitted set SHALL be stated in configuration rather than fixed in the component that reads it, so that what is in force is readable without reading an implementation.

#### Scenario: Generation outside the permitted set is refused

- **WHEN** a key is requested with an algorithm or strength outside the permitted set
- **THEN** generation is refused
- **AND** the refusal names the parameter and the permitted set

#### Scenario: Ingested material with disallowed parameters is refused at receipt

- **WHEN** material is supplied from outside with parameters outside the permitted set
- **THEN** it is refused at the point of receipt
- **AND** it is not written to durable state in any form
- **AND** no later operation can be made to use it

#### Scenario: A newly disallowed parameter raises a condition

- **WHEN** the permitted set is narrowed such that stored items fall outside it
- **THEN** an operator-visible condition names the affected items
- **AND** those items are reported as due for replacement

#### Scenario: A newly disallowed item is not destroyed before its replacement exists

- **WHEN** an item's parameters cease to be permitted and no replacement has been obtained
- **THEN** the item continues to be usable for the connections that require it
- **AND** the condition remains raised until a replacement is in place

### Requirement: Material at rest is unusable to a reader of the store alone

No private key and no protection material SHALL be stored in a form usable by a party holding a complete copy of the durable state. An adversary holding every record, artifact, index, replica, snapshot, and backup of that state, and nothing else, SHALL NOT thereby be able to terminate a connection for any hostname, present the listener's endpoint identity, or decrypt any protected secret. `auth/sidecar-credentials` states this property for stored credential records; it holds here for every other class in the inventory.

Each stored item SHALL be protected independently, so that an item copied out of the store is not usable in another installation and is not usable in place of a different item. Stored form SHALL be integrity-protected: an item whose stored bytes have been altered, truncated, or substituted for another item's SHALL fail closed at use — the platform SHALL refuse to use it, SHALL report the failure distinguishably from the item being absent, and SHALL NOT proceed with a partially recovered value.

Where two items protect identical plaintext material, their stored forms SHALL differ, so that the store does not reveal that two items are the same.

#### Scenario: A full copy of the store yields nothing usable

- **WHEN** an installation is given a complete copy of the durable state, including every replica and backup, and no configured protection material
- **THEN** it recovers no certificate private key and no listener identity material
- **AND** it cannot terminate a connection for any hostname
- **AND** it decrypts no protected secret

#### Scenario: An item is not usable in another installation

- **WHEN** a protected item is copied from one installation's store into another's
- **THEN** the receiving installation fails to use it
- **AND** the failure is distinguishable from the item being absent

#### Scenario: Substituting one item for another fails closed

- **WHEN** the stored bytes of one item are replaced with those of a different item of the same class
- **THEN** use of that item fails
- **AND** no operation proceeds with the substituted value
- **AND** the failure is reported to an operator

#### Scenario: Altered bytes fail closed

- **WHEN** a single byte of a protected item's stored form is altered
- **THEN** recovery of that item fails
- **AND** the platform refuses to use it rather than using a partially recovered value

#### Scenario: Identical material does not produce identical stored bytes

- **WHEN** two items of one class are written protecting the same plaintext material
- **THEN** their stored forms differ
- **AND** neither can be identified as matching the other by comparing stored state

### Requirement: Protection material does not live where the protected material lives

The material that protects stored items SHALL NOT be stored in, derivable from, or recoverable through the store that holds the protected items. It SHALL NOT be embedded in any shipped artifact, image, or package, and SHALL NOT be derivable from any value that accompanies a protected item.

Where the protection material is held outside the platform, the platform SHALL hold only what is needed to request its use, and loss of reachability to it SHALL fail closed: the platform SHALL refuse operations requiring newly recovered material, SHALL continue to serve with material already recovered and in use, and SHALL raise an operator-visible condition for as long as the condition persists.

#### Scenario: The protected store does not contain its own protection

- **WHEN** an installation is started against a complete copy of the durable state with no protection material supplied by configuration
- **THEN** it recovers no protected item
- **AND** it refuses to start rather than obtaining protection material from the state it was given

#### Scenario: A shipped artifact carries no protection material

- **WHEN** a component is started from a shipped artifact with no operator-supplied configuration
- **THEN** it obtains no protection material
- **AND** it refuses to start naming the absent protection material rather than proceeding with a value carried in the artifact

#### Scenario: Loss of protection material fails closed and is announced

- **WHEN** the protection material becomes unreachable
- **THEN** operations requiring newly recovered material are refused
- **AND** connections already served with recovered material continue
- **AND** an operator-visible condition names the unreachable protection material for as long as it persists

### Requirement: No interface returns private key material

No interface the platform exposes — tenant-facing, operator-facing, diagnostic, support, export, backup, or otherwise — SHALL return the private key of any certificate, any protection material, or any value from which either could be reconstructed. An item of material can be used, replaced, and destroyed through the platform's interfaces, and cannot be read back through any of them. `operability/observability` prohibits material in any emitted signal; this requirement governs the interfaces themselves.

There SHALL be no operation whose purpose is to retrieve material, and no operation SHALL return it incidentally: not in a successful response, not in an error message, not in a validation failure, and not in a state dump produced for diagnosis.

An operator holding the highest authority the platform defines SHALL be subject to this identically to a tenant. Authority over material SHALL mean the authority to replace and destroy it, never the authority to read it.

#### Scenario: A tenant cannot read their own key

- **WHEN** a tenant retrieves, enumerates, or exports the certificate state for a hostname they hold
- **THEN** no response contains private key material
- **AND** no response contains a value from which it could be reconstructed

#### Scenario: The highest operator authority cannot read a key

- **WHEN** a principal holding the highest authority the platform defines attempts every operation exposed for certificates and for protection material
- **THEN** none of them returns private key material
- **AND** the ability to replace and destroy the material is unaffected

#### Scenario: A failure naming an item names it by identity only

- **WHEN** an operation using an item of material fails, and a state dump is produced while connections are being terminated with tenant certificates
- **THEN** each identifies the material in use only by class, identity, and version
- **AND** neither contains a fragment of any material

#### Scenario: An export or backup interface yields nothing usable

- **WHEN** every export or backup surface the platform exposes is invoked
- **THEN** no output contains usable private key material
- **AND** restoring the output into a separate installation yields no usable material

### Requirement: Material of a non-yielding class is used only through operations that do not yield it

The inventory SHALL classify each class as one a holder can obtain or one a holder can only have operations performed with. Every class that protects other material or signs a value the platform issues — root material, every derivation of it, the material that authenticates distribution between instances, and any signing key — SHALL be non-yielding: a component entitled to have an operation performed with such an item SHALL NOT thereby be able to obtain the item.

Where a class must be held by a serving instance in order to terminate a connection, the inventory SHALL say so, and that material SHALL be bounded by the requirements governing holders and distribution rather than by this one. The inventory SHALL NOT mark a class non-yielding while any component obtains it, so that the distinction is enforceable rather than nominal.

Compromise of a component entitled to request operations with a non-yielding item SHALL NOT yield the item: an adversary in that position SHALL be able to cause operations for as long as the entitlement stands, and SHALL NOT be able to extract material that outlives revocation of that entitlement. Revoking an entitlement SHALL take effect within a configured bound and SHALL be observable.

#### Scenario: An entitled caller cannot obtain a non-yielding item

- **WHEN** a component entitled to request operations with a non-yielding item attempts to obtain the item itself through every interface available to it
- **THEN** none returns the material
- **AND** the component's ability to request operations is unaffected

#### Scenario: The inventory does not overstate what it protects

- **WHEN** every class marked non-yielding is compared against the classes serving instances actually hold
- **THEN** no class marked non-yielding is held by any component
- **AND** every class a serving instance holds is marked as one a holder obtains

#### Scenario: Revoking an entitlement stops use within a bounded time

- **WHEN** a component's entitlement to request operations with an item is revoked
- **THEN** subsequent requests from that component are refused within the configured bound
- **AND** the refusal is recorded

#### Scenario: Revocation is not survivable by extracted material

- **WHEN** a component's entitlement is revoked
- **THEN** no operation the component can subsequently perform uses that item
- **AND** connections it established before revocation are not required to be terminated for this reason alone

### Requirement: Acquisition and use are bound to a purpose and an identity

Every acquisition of an item and every use of it SHALL be bound to the purpose and the identity the item was generated for, and an acquisition or use naming a different purpose or identity SHALL be refused. Material generated to terminate connections for one hostname SHALL NOT be usable to terminate connections for another, to protect a stored secret, to authenticate distribution between instances, or to sign a value the platform issues.

Binding SHALL be enforced at the moment of use and not only at the moment of acquisition or derivation, so that a component that legitimately acquired an item for one identity cannot redirect it to another.

#### Scenario: An item is refused for a different hostname

- **WHEN** an operation requests use of one hostname's material while naming a different hostname as the identity
- **THEN** the operation is refused
- **AND** the refusal is recorded with both the item and the mismatched identity

#### Scenario: An item is refused for a different purpose

- **WHEN** an operation requests use of a certificate's material for the purpose of protecting a stored secret
- **THEN** the operation is refused
- **AND** no protected record is produced

#### Scenario: An acquired item cannot be redirected

- **WHEN** a component that legitimately acquired an item for one identity applies it to a different identity
- **THEN** the use is refused
- **AND** the refusal does not depend on the component's entitlement to the item it acquired

#### Scenario: Distribution material is not usable as protection material

- **WHEN** the material used to authenticate distribution between instances is applied to a protected stored record
- **THEN** it does not recover that record
- **AND** the failure is recorded with both purposes

### Requirement: The material presented for a connection is that of the selected item and of no other

`routing/custom-domains` owns which item is selected for a requested hostname and the refusal of a hostname with no verified claim; that selection is taken as given. This requirement governs the material behind it: the material presented SHALL be the material of the selected item, belonging to the tenant to whom that hostname is currently verified, and SHALL NOT be the material of any other item.

Where the selected item's material cannot be recovered — because the protection material is unreachable, because the stored form fails its integrity check, or because the item has been destroyed — the connection SHALL be refused with that cause recorded. The platform SHALL NOT substitute a default item, the most recently used item, the platform's own material, or any other tenant's material.

Where a hostname's verification has moved from one tenant to another, the material used for it SHALL be the material of the tenant currently holding it, from the instant the transition takes effect, with no window in which the previous tenant's material is presented.

#### Scenario: Unrecoverable material refuses the connection rather than substituting

- **WHEN** the material for the selected item cannot be recovered
- **THEN** the connection is refused with the recovery failure as the recorded cause
- **AND** no other item's material is presented
- **AND** the cause is distinguishable from the hostname having no verified claim

#### Scenario: Reassignment leaves no window

- **WHEN** a hostname's verification moves from one tenant to another while connections are being attempted continuously
- **THEN** every connection established after the transition uses the new tenant's material
- **AND** no connection after the transition uses the previous tenant's material

#### Scenario: Selection under contention does not cross items

- **WHEN** many connections for many tenants' hostnames are established concurrently under sustained contention, across every instance
- **THEN** every connection is terminated with the material of the item selected for the hostname it requested
- **AND** no connection is terminated with material belonging to another item

### Requirement: No operation performed on one tenant's behalf reaches another tenant's material

Every operation over custody-managed material SHALL be scoped to the tenancy of the acting principal. `tenancy/isolation` owns scoping and the indistinguishability of a refusal, and is taken as given; this requirement applies it to material. An operation performed on one tenant's behalf SHALL NOT use, replace, destroy, enumerate, or reveal the existence of material belonging to another tenant, whether the other tenant's item is named directly, named by a hostname belonging to it, or named by an identifier the caller learned elsewhere.

A tenant SHALL NOT be able to cause replacement or destruction of the platform's own material, of another tenant's material, or of any class whose rotation authority is an operator.

#### Scenario: A tenant cannot destroy another tenant's material

- **WHEN** a tenant requests destruction of material for a hostname held by another tenant
- **THEN** the request is refused
- **AND** the other tenant's material is unchanged and still usable
- **AND** the attempt is recorded in the acting tenant's scope without naming the other tenancy

#### Scenario: A tenant cannot replace platform or operator-authority material

- **WHEN** a tenant requests replacement of the platform's own material or of a class whose rotation authority is an operator
- **THEN** the request is refused
- **AND** the material is unchanged

#### Scenario: Enumeration is scoped

- **WHEN** a tenant enumerates the custody state available to them
- **THEN** only items belonging to their own tenancy are returned
- **AND** no item belonging to another tenancy is returned or counted

### Requirement: The platform's own material is a separate class from any tenant's

The material the platform presents for its control surface, for the hostnames on which it serves traffic on its own behalf, and for the endpoint the tunnel listener advertises SHALL each be a distinct class from tenant material, with its own rotation authority and its own holder set.

Compromise handling of these classes SHALL be independent: forcing replacement of every tenant item SHALL NOT replace the platform's own, and forcing replacement of the platform's own SHALL NOT replace any tenant's. Replacement of one class SHALL NOT interrupt service that depends on another.

#### Scenario: Forced replacement of one class does not touch the other

- **WHEN** an operator forces replacement of every tenant certificate item
- **THEN** the platform's own material and the listener's endpoint identity are unchanged
- **AND** the report distinguishes the classes

#### Scenario: Replacing the platform's own class does not disturb tenant serving

- **WHEN** the platform's own material is replaced while tenant hostnames are served continuously
- **THEN** no tenant item is replaced
- **AND** no connection for a tenant hostname is dropped or refused as a consequence

#### Scenario: A class boundary cannot be crossed by naming

- **WHEN** an operation names a tenant item while requesting the platform's own class, and names the platform's own item while requesting a tenant class
- **THEN** both are refused
- **AND** neither item is used, replaced, or destroyed

### Requirement: Material never crosses the tunnel and never leaves its trust boundary

No item of custody-managed material SHALL be carried in any tunnel frame, in any field of the wire contract, or in any payload sent to a sidecar. A certificate private key held by the platform SHALL NOT be sent to a tenant, to a sidecar, or to a backend, on request or otherwise.

Material held by a sidecar for protecting its own stored credential SHALL remain in the tenant's own infrastructure and SHALL NOT be sent to the platform, requested by the platform, or recoverable by the platform from anything the sidecar transmits. The platform SHALL expose no operation that asks a sidecar for it.

A request that would result in material crossing either boundary SHALL be refused with a cause naming the boundary, and SHALL be recorded.

#### Scenario: No frame carries material

- **WHEN** every frame exchanged for a hostname whose connections are being terminated with a tenant certificate is captured
- **THEN** no frame contains private key material
- **AND** no field of the wire contract is populated with it

#### Scenario: A request to send a key to a sidecar is refused

- **WHEN** a request is made that would deliver a certificate private key to a sidecar or a backend
- **THEN** it is refused with a cause naming the boundary
- **AND** the attempt is recorded

#### Scenario: The platform cannot obtain a sidecar's protection material

- **WHEN** everything a sidecar transmits during establishment and operation is captured, and every operation the platform exposes toward a sidecar is invoked
- **THEN** the material protecting the sidecar's stored credential is not present in the capture
- **AND** no operation asks the sidecar to supply it

### Requirement: Where material is supplied from outside, custody begins at receipt

Where the platform accepts material supplied from outside — operator-supplied protection material, or certificate material supplied through any surface that accepts it — every obligation of this capability SHALL apply from the instant of receipt. The supplied value SHALL be protected before the operation that received it completes, and SHALL NOT be retained anywhere on the receiving path: not in a record of the request, not in an error report, and not in a retry of the receiving operation.

Receipt SHALL validate that the supplied material and any certificate it accompanies correspond, and that such a certificate names only hostnames verified to the supplying tenant. Where either check fails, the material SHALL be refused and nothing SHALL be written.

The receiving operation SHALL NOT echo any part of the supplied value back to the caller, and SHALL NOT be repeatable as a way to learn whether material is already present for the named item.

#### Scenario: Supplied material is protected before the operation completes

- **WHEN** material is supplied from outside
- **AND** the durable state is examined immediately after the operation completes
- **THEN** it is present only in protected form
- **AND** it is present in no other location

#### Scenario: The receiving path retains nothing

- **WHEN** material is supplied from outside
- **THEN** no record of the request, measurement, or error report produced by that operation contains the supplied value
- **AND** a retry of the operation does not surface the previously supplied value

#### Scenario: Mismatched material is refused

- **WHEN** supplied material does not correspond to the certificate it accompanies
- **THEN** the operation is refused with a cause naming the mismatch
- **AND** nothing is written to durable state

#### Scenario: A certificate naming an unverified hostname is refused

- **WHEN** a certificate is supplied naming a hostname not verified to the supplying tenant
- **THEN** the operation is refused
- **AND** the refusal does not reveal whether that hostname is verified to anyone else

#### Scenario: The receiving operation is not an oracle

- **WHEN** the receiving operation is invoked for an item that already holds material and for one that does not
- **THEN** the two responses are identical in status, code, and content

### Requirement: An instance holds material only for what it serves

`routing/custom-domains` requires certificate material to be shared state, requires a joining instance to serve every already-verified hostname without re-issuing, and requires issuance to happen once rather than once per instance; that is taken as given. This requirement bounds the other direction.

No instance SHALL hold, in memory or on local storage, material for an item it does not serve, and no instance SHALL hold material belonging to another installation. Where an instance ceases to serve an item, its ability to use that item's material SHALL end within a configured bound, and it SHALL retain nothing usable afterwards.

Acquisition SHALL be on the demand of serving. An instance SHALL NOT generate material locally for an item the shared state already holds material for, and SHALL NOT retain locally generated material the shared state does not know about.

#### Scenario: An instance holds nothing for items it does not serve

- **WHEN** an instance's local state and memory are examined while it serves a subset of items
- **THEN** no material for an item outside that subset is present
- **AND** no material for another installation is present

#### Scenario: Ceasing to serve ends the ability to use

- **WHEN** an instance stops serving an item
- **THEN** within the configured bound it can no longer use that item's material
- **AND** nothing usable for that item remains on that instance

#### Scenario: Concurrent demand leaves no orphaned local material

- **WHEN** several instances begin serving the same newly verified hostname concurrently
- **THEN** one item of material is generated for it and every instance uses that one item
- **AND** no instance retains locally generated material for it that the shared state does not hold

### Requirement: Distribution of material between instances is authenticated and does not expose it

Material moving between the shared state and an instance SHALL travel over a path that authenticates both ends and protects the material in transit, and SHALL NOT traverse any path that exposes it: it SHALL NOT be carried on the tunnel, SHALL NOT be carried on any surface that serves tenant traffic, SHALL NOT be carried on an operational or probe surface, and SHALL NOT be written to an intermediate location that outlives the transfer.

An instance that cannot authenticate itself SHALL obtain nothing. An instance whose entitlement has been withdrawn SHALL obtain nothing, including material it previously held. A party that can observe the distribution path SHALL obtain nothing usable.

Failure of a distribution attempt SHALL be reported with a cause distinguishing "not entitled", "could not authenticate the far end", and "could not reach the shared state", because the three demand different operator responses.

#### Scenario: An unauthenticated instance obtains nothing

- **WHEN** a party that cannot authenticate itself as an instance of the installation requests material
- **THEN** it obtains nothing
- **AND** the refusal does not reveal whether the requested item exists

#### Scenario: Observing the distribution path yields nothing

- **WHEN** the distribution path between the shared state and an instance is observed in full during transfer of many items
- **THEN** no item is recoverable from the observation

#### Scenario: A withdrawn instance obtains nothing, including what it held before

- **WHEN** an instance's entitlement is withdrawn and it requests material it previously held
- **THEN** it obtains nothing
- **AND** the attempt is recorded

#### Scenario: Distribution does not use tenant or operational surfaces

- **WHEN** every surface that serves tenant traffic and every operational or probe surface is exercised
- **THEN** none of them can be made to carry material
- **AND** no request on them causes a distribution transfer

#### Scenario: Distribution failures are distinguishable

- **WHEN** distribution fails because the requester is not entitled, because the far end could not be authenticated, and because the shared state was unreachable
- **THEN** the three produce distinguishable causes
- **AND** each is recorded with that cause

### Requirement: An isolated instance generates no replacement and reconciles on recovery

`routing/custom-domains` requires an instance that cannot reach shared state to keep serving what it holds, to request no new certificate, and not to treat unreachability as a lapse of verification. This requirement adds the custody obligations that follow from it.

An instance that cannot reach the shared material SHALL NOT generate a replacement for material it cannot confirm, SHALL NOT destroy material it holds, and SHALL NOT treat unreachability as evidence that material has been destroyed. On regaining reachability it SHALL reconcile before serving further connections with affected material — acquiring material for items it now serves and losing the ability to use material destroyed or replaced while it was isolated — and the reconciliation SHALL be observable.

#### Scenario: An isolated instance generates no replacement

- **WHEN** an instance cannot reach the shared material and holds material it cannot confirm
- **THEN** it generates no replacement material
- **AND** it continues to use the material it already holds for the items it already serves

#### Scenario: Unreachability is not read as destruction

- **WHEN** an instance cannot reach the shared material
- **THEN** it does not destroy the material it holds
- **AND** it does not report the material as destroyed

#### Scenario: Recovery reconciles destruction that happened during isolation

- **WHEN** material is destroyed or replaced while an instance is isolated
- **AND** the instance regains reachability
- **THEN** it can no longer use the outgoing material
- **AND** it serves no connection with it after reconciliation begins
- **AND** the reconciliation is observable

### Requirement: Derivation is purpose-scoped and versioned for every class

`auth/sidecar-credentials` states purpose separation and versioning for credential-protection material. This requirement holds it for every derived class in the inventory, and adds the behaviour on an unidentifiable version.

Material derived for one purpose SHALL be derived such that it is not usable against any other purpose, and such that disclosure of one purpose's material discloses neither any other purpose's material nor the material it was derived from. The purpose SHALL be a fixed part of the derivation rather than a convention applied by callers.

Every derivation SHALL carry a version, and every record protected under derived material SHALL identify the version it was protected under. More than one version SHALL be usable simultaneously, so that derived material can be replaced without invalidating every record at once. Where a version has been retired, records protected under it SHALL fail in the manner the capability owning that class of secret defines, and the retired version SHALL be named as the cause in the audit trail rather than in any response to the presenter.

A record that identifies no version, or a version the installation does not recognise, SHALL fail closed rather than being tried against every version in turn.

#### Scenario: One purpose's material does not work against another class

- **WHEN** material derived for one purpose is applied to a record protected for a different purpose in another class
- **THEN** it does not recover or validate that record
- **AND** the failure is recorded with both purposes

#### Scenario: The purpose is not a caller-supplied convention

- **WHEN** a caller requests derivation naming a purpose other than the one the item is enumerated for
- **THEN** the derivation is refused
- **AND** no material usable against the named purpose is produced

#### Scenario: Two versions are usable at once for a non-credential class

- **WHEN** the derived material protecting a class of stored secret is replaced while records under the prior version are still current
- **THEN** records of both versions are recoverable
- **AND** newly written records are protected under the new version

#### Scenario: An unversioned or unrecognised record fails closed

- **WHEN** a stored record identifies no version, or a version the installation does not recognise
- **THEN** it fails to be recovered
- **AND** no attempt is made against any other version
- **AND** the failure is recorded

### Requirement: Root material is operator-supplied and is not recoverable from anything the platform stores

The material from which every derived class descends SHALL be supplied by operator configuration. The platform SHALL NOT generate it and store it alongside the data it protects, SHALL NOT write it to the durable store, and SHALL NOT include it in any artifact it ships or any output it produces.

The platform SHALL expose no operation that returns the root, derives it from any derived material, or accepts derived material in place of it. Its configured value SHALL be redacted wherever the configuration in force is recorded or reported.

#### Scenario: The root is absent from durable state

- **WHEN** the durable state is searched exhaustively for the root material
- **THEN** it is not present in any form
- **AND** an installation given only that state and no configured root recovers no protected record

#### Scenario: Derived material is not accepted in place of the root

- **WHEN** an installation is configured with derived material where the root is expected
- **THEN** it refuses to start naming the root as absent or invalid
- **AND** no operation it exposes returns or reconstructs the root from derived material

#### Scenario: The recorded configuration redacts the root

- **WHEN** the configuration in force is recorded at startup or reported on demand
- **THEN** the root material is redacted
- **AND** the redaction does not reveal its length or any part of its value

### Requirement: There is no escrow or recovery path, and unrecoverable material is reported rather than worked around

The platform SHALL provide no escrow, recovery, break-glass, or support path that yields material or reconstructs it from anything the platform holds. No documented emergency procedure SHALL depend on one, and no configuration SHALL enable one.

Where material is permanently unrecoverable — the protection material for it is gone, or its stored form fails integrity permanently — the platform SHALL report each affected item as unrecoverable, distinguishably from absent and from destroyed, and SHALL raise an operator-visible condition naming them. It SHALL NOT silently generate a replacement in place of the unrecoverable item, SHALL NOT treat the affected records as absent, and SHALL NOT serve with a partially recovered value.

Recovery from that state SHALL be through the ordinary generation and issuance path, producing new material and leaving records protected under the lost material unrecoverable and reported as such.

#### Scenario: No interface offers a recovery of material

- **WHEN** every operation the platform exposes to its highest operator authority is invoked
- **THEN** none returns material, reconstructs it, or offers to
- **AND** no configuration setting enables such an operation

#### Scenario: Permanently unrecoverable material is reported, not silently replaced

- **WHEN** the protection material for a class is permanently lost
- **THEN** each affected item is reported as unrecoverable, distinguishably from absent and from destroyed
- **AND** no replacement is generated for it without an explicit operator action
- **AND** an operator-visible condition names the affected items

#### Scenario: Records under lost material are not treated as absent

- **WHEN** a record protected under permanently lost material is presented
- **THEN** it fails closed with the unrecoverable cause recorded
- **AND** it is not treated as a record that never existed

#### Scenario: Recovery uses the ordinary path

- **WHEN** an operator recovers an item whose material was permanently lost
- **THEN** new material is generated through the ordinary path
- **AND** the lost material is not reconstructed
- **AND** the items that depended on it remain reported as unrecoverable

### Requirement: Required material is validated before service begins

Before any listener, tunnel endpoint, probe surface, or control surface accepts a connection, the installation SHALL validate every inventory entry marked as required to start: that it is present, that it is recoverable with the configured protection material, that it satisfies the configured parameter set, and that it can actually perform the operation its purpose requires.

Where a certificate accompanies material, validation SHALL confirm that the two correspond, and SHALL refuse to start on a mismatch rather than discovering it at the first connection. Validation SHALL NOT be satisfied by the material merely being present or parseable.

Validation SHALL produce no material in any output: neither the refusal, nor the successful startup record, nor any measurement emitted during validation SHALL contain it.

#### Scenario: Unrecoverable required material prevents start

- **WHEN** required material is present but cannot be recovered with the configured protection material
- **THEN** the installation refuses to start
- **AND** the refusal names that item and the nature of the failure
- **AND** no connection is accepted at any point

#### Scenario: A key that cannot perform its operation prevents start

- **WHEN** required material is present and recoverable but cannot perform the operation its purpose requires
- **THEN** the installation refuses to start naming that item
- **AND** the failure is not deferred to the first connection

#### Scenario: A certificate and key that do not correspond prevent start

- **WHEN** a required certificate and the material accompanying it do not correspond
- **THEN** the installation refuses to start with a cause naming the mismatch
- **AND** the refusal contains no fragment of the material

#### Scenario: Validation precedes accepting anything

- **WHEN** the installation refuses to start on custody validation
- **THEN** no listener, tunnel endpoint, probe surface, or control surface accepted a connection at any point

### Requirement: A published, absent, or undersized configured value is refused for every custody class

`auth/sidecar-credentials` requires this for the configuration from which credential-protection material is derived. This requirement holds it for every configured item of material in the inventory, including root material, the platform's own certificate material, the listener's endpoint identity, and distribution material.

The installation SHALL refuse to start where any configured item of material equals a value published in the project's own example, development, or test configuration, published in its documentation, or shipped in any artifact. The refusal SHALL name that specific cause rather than reporting the value as merely invalid.

It SHALL likewise refuse a value shorter than the configured minimum for its class, reporting the class, the minimum, and the observed length — never the value. A required item absent from configuration SHALL be a refusal and SHALL NOT be replaced by a generated or default value. Every custody item that fails validation SHALL be named in the single startup refusal `operability/observability` requires, rather than one item per restart.

#### Scenario: A published development value is refused for a non-credential class

- **WHEN** the configured root material, or the configured distribution material, equals a value published in the project's own example or development configuration
- **THEN** the installation refuses to start
- **AND** the refusal names that specific cause and that class

#### Scenario: A short value is refused with its length and not its content

- **WHEN** a configured item of any custody class is shorter than the configured minimum for its class
- **THEN** the installation refuses to start
- **AND** the refusal states the class, the minimum, and the observed length
- **AND** it does not state the value

#### Scenario: A missing item is a failure rather than a default

- **WHEN** a required item of material is absent from configuration
- **THEN** the installation refuses to start naming that item and its class
- **AND** no value is generated or substituted for it

### Requirement: A non-production configuration neither weakens protection nor yields usable material

The parameters that protect material SHALL be identical, for every class in the inventory, in every configuration in which the system's behaviour is asserted. No configuration SHALL reduce the strength of protection, skip integrity checking, substitute a fixed value for generated material, or disable a custody check, because the property under test would then be erased by the configuration under which it is tested.

A copy of durable state restored into a separate environment SHALL yield no usable material without that environment holding the original protection material, and there SHALL be no path by which production material becomes usable in a non-production environment.

#### Scenario: No configuration weakens protection for any class

- **WHEN** every configuration the project ships is compared against the production configuration, class by class
- **THEN** the protection parameters for every class are identical
- **AND** no custody check is disabled in any of them

#### Scenario: A restored copy yields nothing without the original protection

- **WHEN** a copy of production durable state is restored into a separate environment with different protection material
- **THEN** no item is recoverable there
- **AND** no connection can be terminated for any hostname in that environment

#### Scenario: Fixed material is not substituted anywhere

- **WHEN** material is generated under every configuration the project ships
- **THEN** no configuration produces a fixed or predictable value
- **AND** repeated generation under each produces distinct values

### Requirement: Each class of material can be replaced without interrupting service

Every class in the inventory SHALL be replaceable while the installation continues to serve. Replacement SHALL NOT require a restart, SHALL NOT require every instance to be replaced at once, and SHALL NOT drop an established connection or an in-flight exchange.

During a replacement window both the outgoing and incoming material SHALL be usable: new work SHALL use the incoming material, work already bound to the outgoing material SHALL complete with it, and records protected under the outgoing material SHALL remain recoverable until they have been re-protected or have expired.

Incoming material SHALL be validated to the same standard as material required at start before it is put in force. Where validation fails, the replacement SHALL be refused, the outgoing material SHALL remain in force, and the failure SHALL be recorded and raised. The outgoing material SHALL be destroyed only after nothing depends on it, and the platform SHALL be able to state what still depends on it at any moment during the window.

#### Scenario: Replacement drops no connection

- **WHEN** a class of material is replaced while traffic is served continuously
- **THEN** no established connection is dropped
- **AND** no in-flight exchange fails for a reason attributable to the replacement

#### Scenario: Both are usable during the window

- **WHEN** a class of material is replaced
- **THEN** new work uses the incoming material
- **AND** records protected under the outgoing material remain recoverable until re-protected or expired

#### Scenario: Invalid incoming material does not displace the outgoing material

- **WHEN** incoming material fails the validation required of material at start
- **THEN** the replacement is refused
- **AND** the outgoing material remains in force and serving continues with it
- **AND** the failure is recorded and raised to an operator

#### Scenario: Instances are not required to replace simultaneously

- **WHEN** a class of material is replaced while instances observe the change at different times
- **THEN** every instance serves correctly throughout
- **AND** no request is refused because two instances disagree about which material is current

#### Scenario: The outgoing material is not destroyed while it is depended on

- **WHEN** the outgoing material still protects recoverable records or still terminates established connections
- **THEN** it is not destroyed
- **AND** the platform can state what still depends on it

### Requirement: A certificate replacement carries fresh material

Each issuance or renewal of a certificate SHALL use freshly generated material rather than reusing the material of the certificate it replaces. Material SHALL NOT be carried across a hostname's removal and re-claim, and SHALL NOT be carried from one tenant to another when a hostname changes hands.

An incoming certificate that does not correspond to the material generated for it SHALL NOT be put in force, and the outgoing certificate and its material SHALL remain in use.

Connections established with the outgoing certificate SHALL continue with the outgoing material until they end; connections established after the replacement takes effect SHALL use the incoming material. The outgoing material SHALL be destroyed once no established connection depends on it and the outgoing certificate is no longer presented anywhere.

#### Scenario: Renewal generates new material

- **WHEN** a certificate is renewed for a hostname
- **THEN** the material behind the replacement differs from the material behind the certificate it replaces
- **AND** the outgoing material is not reused for any other item

#### Scenario: A non-corresponding certificate is not put in force

- **WHEN** an obtained certificate does not correspond to the material generated for its request
- **THEN** it is not put in force
- **AND** the outgoing certificate and material continue to be presented
- **AND** the mismatch is recorded and raised to an operator

#### Scenario: Established connections finish on the outgoing material

- **WHEN** a certificate is renewed while connections for its hostname are established
- **THEN** those connections continue without interruption
- **AND** connections established afterwards use the incoming material

#### Scenario: Re-claiming a hostname does not restore its old material

- **WHEN** a hostname is removed and later claimed and verified again, whether by the same tenant or a different one
- **THEN** the material used for it is newly generated
- **AND** the material held before removal is not usable for it

#### Scenario: Outgoing material is destroyed after nothing depends on it

- **WHEN** every connection established with the outgoing certificate has ended and it is presented nowhere
- **THEN** the outgoing material is destroyed
- **AND** the destruction is recorded

### Requirement: Rotation progress and completion are observable per class

For each class, the platform SHALL make observable whether a replacement is in progress, how many items or records remain on the outgoing material, the time the current replacement began, and which holders still use the outgoing material.

A replacement SHALL be reported as complete only when nothing depends on the outgoing material, and completion SHALL be observable without inspecting the material. A replacement that has been started SHALL be resumable after a restart or an instance failure, and SHALL NOT restart from the beginning or lose track of what has already been replaced.

#### Scenario: Remaining dependants are countable

- **WHEN** a replacement is in progress for a class protecting many records
- **THEN** the count of records still on the outgoing material is observable
- **AND** it decreases as re-protection proceeds

#### Scenario: Completion means nothing depends on the outgoing material

- **WHEN** a replacement is reported complete
- **THEN** no record and no connection depends on the outgoing material
- **AND** the report is available without inspecting any material

#### Scenario: A replacement resumes after a restart

- **WHEN** an instance performing a replacement restarts partway through
- **THEN** the replacement resumes from where it stopped
- **AND** records already re-protected are not re-protected again
- **AND** no record is skipped

#### Scenario: An incomplete replacement is not reported as complete

- **WHEN** a replacement is queried while records remain on the outgoing material
- **THEN** it is reported as in progress with the remaining count
- **AND** it is not reported as complete

### Requirement: A rotation that stalls is surfaced

Where a replacement has been in progress longer than a configured bound, has made no progress within a configured bound, or cannot complete because dependants remain on the outgoing material, the platform SHALL raise an operator-visible condition naming the class, when the replacement began, what remains, and why it cannot complete.

The condition SHALL persist until the replacement completes or is abandoned, and SHALL NOT be cleared by the passage of time. A stalled replacement SHALL NOT cause the outgoing material to be destroyed, and SHALL NOT cause the incoming material to be withdrawn.

Abandoning a replacement SHALL be an explicit operator action, SHALL be recorded, and SHALL leave the class on a stated one of the two materials rather than in an indeterminate state.

#### Scenario: A stalled replacement raises a condition

- **WHEN** a replacement makes no progress for longer than the configured bound
- **THEN** an operator-visible condition names the class, the start time, what remains, and the reason
- **AND** it persists until the replacement completes or is abandoned

#### Scenario: A stall does not destroy either material

- **WHEN** a replacement is stalled
- **THEN** the outgoing material is not destroyed
- **AND** the incoming material is not withdrawn
- **AND** serving continues with both usable

#### Scenario: Abandonment is explicit and leaves a stated state

- **WHEN** an operator abandons a stalled replacement
- **THEN** the class is left on one stated material
- **AND** the abandonment is recorded with the acting principal and the outcome

#### Scenario: The condition is not cleared by time alone

- **WHEN** a stalled replacement remains stalled for longer than any configured interval in force
- **THEN** the condition remains raised
- **AND** it is not superseded or aged out

### Requirement: Replacing root material re-protects records rather than invalidating them

Where root material is replaced, every purpose derived from it SHALL be re-derived, and every record protected under the outgoing derivation SHALL be re-protected under the incoming one. Records SHALL NOT be invalidated en masse as a consequence of the replacement.

Both root generations SHALL be usable during the window, and every protected record SHALL identify which generation protected it. Re-protection SHALL proceed as bounded work whose progress is observable, and SHALL be resumable. A record that cannot be re-protected SHALL be named individually rather than skipped silently, and SHALL leave the replacement incomplete. The outgoing root SHALL be destroyable only once the count of records still protected under it is zero, and that count SHALL be observable.

#### Scenario: Root replacement invalidates no record

- **WHEN** root material is replaced while records protected under the outgoing derivation are current
- **THEN** every such record remains usable throughout
- **AND** none is invalidated as a consequence of the replacement

#### Scenario: Records identify their generation

- **WHEN** protected records exist from before and after a root replacement
- **THEN** each identifies the generation under which it was protected
- **AND** each is recovered under that generation

#### Scenario: A record that cannot be re-protected is named

- **WHEN** re-protection of a record fails during a root replacement
- **THEN** that record is named in the operator-visible condition
- **AND** the replacement is not reported complete
- **AND** the record is not skipped or discarded

#### Scenario: The outgoing root cannot be destroyed while records depend on it

- **WHEN** destruction of the outgoing root is requested while records protected under it remain
- **THEN** the request is refused
- **AND** the refusal states the count of records still depending on it

#### Scenario: Re-protection does not degrade serving beyond the declared tolerance

- **WHEN** re-protection of many records proceeds under load
- **THEN** serving remains within the interference tolerance the installation declares
- **AND** the progress of re-protection is observable throughout

### Requirement: An operator can force immediate replacement of a class

An operator SHALL be able to force replacement of a whole class of material, or of a single named item, without waiting for its scheduled replacement and without restarting the installation. Forcing replacement SHALL be available for every class in the inventory, including root material, the platform's own material, and the listener's endpoint identity.

The operator SHALL be able to choose explicitly between retaining the outgoing material for the ordinary overlap window and withdrawing it immediately. The consequence of immediate withdrawal SHALL be stated at the point of the choice rather than discovered afterwards, and SHALL name what depends on the outgoing material, including any peer population a capability owning that class declares depends on an overlap. Immediate withdrawal SHALL NOT be the default.

A forced replacement SHALL be recorded with the acting principal, the class or item, the reason supplied, and whether the outgoing material was withdrawn immediately.

#### Scenario: A forced replacement takes effect without a restart

- **WHEN** an operator forces replacement of a class
- **THEN** new work uses the incoming material without any component being restarted
- **AND** the action is recorded with the acting principal and the reason

#### Scenario: Immediate withdrawal is explicit and its consequence is stated

- **WHEN** an operator forces replacement of the listener's endpoint identity and elects to withdraw the outgoing material immediately
- **THEN** the consequence for work and for peers depending on the outgoing material is stated before the action takes effect
- **AND** the election is recorded

#### Scenario: Overlap is retained by default

- **WHEN** an operator forces replacement without electing immediate withdrawal
- **THEN** the outgoing material remains usable for the ordinary window
- **AND** no established connection is dropped

#### Scenario: Every class can be forced

- **WHEN** forced replacement is requested in turn for every class in the inventory
- **THEN** each is replaceable
- **AND** no class is exempt

#### Scenario: A tenant cannot force a replacement outside their scope

- **WHEN** a tenant requests forced replacement of a class or of an item outside their own tenancy
- **THEN** the request is refused
- **AND** the material is unchanged

### Requirement: A forced replacement reports what was replaced and what still uses the outgoing material

A forced replacement SHALL produce a report enumerating what was replaced, what was not, and what still depends on the outgoing material — by class, item identity, and holder — with a stated reason for anything not replaced. The report SHALL be available while the replacement is in progress and not only at its end.

Where a holder cannot be reached, the report SHALL name it as unreached rather than counting it as replaced, and the corresponding operator-visible condition SHALL persist until it is reached or removed from the installation. A replacement SHALL NOT be reported as complete while any holder is unreached.

The report SHALL identify material by class, identity, version, and holder only, and SHALL contain no material.

#### Scenario: The report enumerates outstanding dependants

- **WHEN** a forced replacement is in progress
- **THEN** the report names every item still on the outgoing material and every holder still using it
- **AND** the enumeration is available before the replacement completes

#### Scenario: An unreached holder is not counted as replaced

- **WHEN** a holder cannot be reached during a forced replacement
- **THEN** the report names it as unreached
- **AND** the replacement is not reported as complete
- **AND** an operator-visible condition persists until it is reached or removed

#### Scenario: Anything not replaced carries a reason

- **WHEN** an item is not replaced during a forced replacement
- **THEN** the report states why
- **AND** the reason distinguishes an unreached holder from a refused replacement

#### Scenario: The report carries no material

- **WHEN** a forced-replacement report is produced
- **THEN** it identifies material by class, identity, version, and holder only
- **AND** it contains no fragment of any material

### Requirement: Material for a removed hostname or a revoked credential is destroyed

Where a hostname claim is removed, where a hostname's verification lapses permanently, where a certificate is superseded and no longer presented, or where a credential is revoked or reaped, the material associated with it SHALL be destroyed within a configured bound rather than left in place indefinitely. `auth/sidecar-credentials` owns the reaping of the credential record; this requirement covers the material behind it.

Destruction SHALL NOT occur while an established connection or an in-flight exchange still depends on the material; it SHALL wait for that dependency to end, bounded by the configured drain, and the wait SHALL be observable. Once destruction has occurred, no operation SHALL be able to use the material, and the platform SHALL report the item as destroyed rather than as absent, so that the difference between "never existed" and "destroyed" remains visible to an operator.

Removal of a hostname claim SHALL NOT leave material with no owning claim, and material with no owning claim, certificate, or credential SHALL be reported as orphaned and destroyed within the configured bound.

#### Scenario: Removing a claim destroys its material

- **WHEN** a tenant removes a hostname claim
- **THEN** the material for that hostname is destroyed within the configured bound
- **AND** it can no longer be used for any connection

#### Scenario: Destruction waits for dependants, bounded

- **WHEN** material is due for destruction while connections still depend on it
- **THEN** destruction waits until those connections end or the configured drain elapses
- **AND** the wait is observable to an operator

#### Scenario: Orphaned material is found and destroyed

- **WHEN** material exists for which no claim, certificate, or credential remains
- **THEN** it is reported as orphaned
- **AND** it is destroyed within the configured bound

#### Scenario: A destroyed item is reported as destroyed

- **WHEN** an operator inspects the custody state of a destroyed item
- **THEN** it is reported as destroyed rather than as never having existed
- **AND** the report contains no material

### Requirement: Destruction is verifiable and complete across every holder

Destruction SHALL be verifiable rather than assumed. The platform SHALL be able to state, for a destroyed item, that every holder that could use it can no longer use it, and SHALL enumerate any holder for which that has not yet been confirmed.

Destruction SHALL propagate to the shared state, to every instance that held the item, and to every derived copy the platform is responsible for. Where a holder cannot be reached, the item SHALL NOT be reported as destroyed; it SHALL be reported as destruction-pending, naming the unreached holders, and an operator-visible condition SHALL persist until each is reached or removed from the installation.

A verification that a destroyed item is unusable SHALL be performed by attempting the operation the item served and observing refusal, not by observing the absence of a stored record.

#### Scenario: Destruction is confirmed at every holder

- **WHEN** an item is destroyed while several instances held it
- **THEN** each instance is confirmed unable to use it
- **AND** the confirmation is recorded per holder

#### Scenario: An unreached holder leaves destruction pending

- **WHEN** an instance that held a destroyed item cannot be reached
- **THEN** the item is reported as destruction-pending naming that instance
- **AND** it is not reported as destroyed
- **AND** an operator-visible condition persists until that instance is reached or removed

#### Scenario: Verification exercises the operation, not the record

- **WHEN** destruction of an item is verified
- **THEN** the verification attempts the operation the item served and observes refusal
- **AND** it does not rely on the absence of a stored record

#### Scenario: Destruction reaches replicas and derived copies

- **WHEN** an item is destroyed
- **THEN** it is unusable through every replica and derived copy the platform is responsible for
- **AND** any copy outside the platform's responsibility is named in the report rather than assumed destroyed

### Requirement: A destroyed item does not come back

Restoring shared state, restoring a backup, rejoining an isolated instance, re-claiming a released hostname, or re-issuing a revoked credential SHALL NOT make destroyed material usable again.

An instance rejoining after isolation SHALL lose the ability to use anything destroyed while it was isolated, before it serves a connection with it. A restore that predates a destruction SHALL NOT reinstate the destroyed material as usable; where a restore would do so, the platform SHALL refuse to use the reinstated item and SHALL raise an operator-visible condition naming it.

#### Scenario: A rejoining instance loses destroyed material first

- **WHEN** an instance rejoins after material was destroyed during its isolation
- **THEN** it cannot use that material
- **AND** it does not terminate any connection with it after rejoining

#### Scenario: A restore does not reinstate destroyed material

- **WHEN** shared state is restored from a point before a destruction
- **THEN** the destroyed material is not usable
- **AND** an operator-visible condition names the reinstated item

#### Scenario: Re-issuing a credential does not recover destroyed protection

- **WHEN** a credential is revoked, its material destroyed, and a replacement issued
- **THEN** the replacement is protected under current material
- **AND** the destroyed material is not recovered

### Requirement: Material is not recoverable from a holder after use

A holder SHALL retain material in memory only for as long as it is needed to serve, and SHALL clear it when released. Clearing SHALL be safe against concurrent use: a holder SHALL NOT clear material another operation is using, and a release racing an in-flight operation SHALL either complete the operation or refuse it, never operate on partially cleared material.

Material SHALL NOT be present in any artifact a holder produces on failure — a crash artifact, a memory dump, a stack trace, or a saved diagnostic — and where such artifacts are produced by the environment rather than by the holder, the platform SHALL state that as a documented residual exposure rather than implying a property it does not have.

State retained for a connection SHALL carry only what is needed to serve and to revoke — the identity, class, and version of the material used — and SHALL NOT retain the material.

#### Scenario: Released material is cleared

- **WHEN** a holder releases an item after use
- **THEN** the material is no longer present in the holder's retained state
- **AND** a subsequent operation requires acquiring it again

#### Scenario: Release racing use does not corrupt an operation

- **WHEN** a release and an operation using the same item occur concurrently, repeatedly
- **THEN** each operation either completes correctly or is refused
- **AND** no operation proceeds on partially cleared material

#### Scenario: Failure artifacts carry no material

- **WHEN** a holder fails while using material and produces a crash artifact or diagnostic
- **THEN** the artifact contains no material
- **AND** any residual exposure produced by the environment rather than the holder is documented rather than implied to be absent

#### Scenario: Connection state carries identity, not material

- **WHEN** the state retained for an established connection terminated with a tenant certificate is examined
- **THEN** it names the identity, class, and version of the material used
- **AND** it does not contain the material

### Requirement: Use of material is accountable for every class

Use of custody-managed material SHALL be accountable at a granularity stated per class: each use of a certificate's material to establish a connection SHALL be attributable, and for classes used at a rate that makes per-use records unusable, use SHALL be accounted as a measurement per class, item, and version rather than dropped. Every class in the inventory SHALL be covered by one or the other, so that no class is unaccounted for.

A refused use SHALL be recorded with its specific cause, including a use refused for a purpose mismatch, an identity mismatch, an unentitled requester, a retired version, an unrecoverable item, and a destroyed item. A refused use SHALL be recorded even where the requester is not authenticated.

#### Scenario: Every class is accounted for one way or the other

- **WHEN** the accounting for use is compared against the custody inventory
- **THEN** every class is covered either by attributable per-use records or by a per-class measurement
- **AND** no class is uncovered

#### Scenario: A refused use is recorded with its cause

- **WHEN** uses are refused for a purpose mismatch, an identity mismatch, an unentitled requester, a retired version, an unrecoverable item, and a destroyed item
- **THEN** each is recorded with that specific cause
- **AND** the causes are distinguishable from one another

#### Scenario: An unauthenticated attempt is still recorded

- **WHEN** an unauthenticated party attempts to cause use of an item
- **THEN** the refusal is recorded with the observed source
- **AND** the record does not reveal whether the item exists

### Requirement: Replacement and destruction are recorded, and the records outlive the material

Every replacement and every destruction SHALL produce a record stating the acting principal, the class, the item identity, the version replaced and the version put in force, the reason where one was supplied, the time, and the outcome. A record SHALL be produced whether the outcome was success, refusal, or a stall.

These records SHALL outlive the material they describe: destroying material SHALL NOT remove, alter, or truncate any record describing it, so that an item destroyed leaves an account of its existence, its use, and its destruction. The retention period for those records is not stated by this capability and SHALL NOT be inferred from the lifetime of the material.

Records SHALL be readable within the scope of the principal reading them: a tenant SHALL read records concerning their own material and no others; a record of an operator action SHALL be distinguishable from a record of a tenant action. No record SHALL contain material.

#### Scenario: A replacement is recorded end to end

- **WHEN** a class of material is replaced
- **THEN** a record names the acting principal, the class, the versions out and in, the time, and the outcome
- **AND** the record contains no material

#### Scenario: Destroying material does not remove its records

- **WHEN** an item is destroyed and its records are read afterwards
- **THEN** the records of its creation, replacement, and destruction remain readable unchanged
- **AND** they identify it without containing it

#### Scenario: A refused replacement is recorded

- **WHEN** a replacement is refused
- **THEN** a record names the acting principal, the class, the attempted change, and the refusal
- **AND** the material is unchanged

#### Scenario: Records are scoped to the reader

- **WHEN** a tenant reads custody records
- **THEN** only records concerning their own material are returned
- **AND** no record concerning another tenancy is returned or counted

### Requirement: The custody state of every item is enumerable without the material

An operator SHALL be able to enumerate, for every item the installation holds: its class, its identity, its owning tenancy where it has one, the version or generation in force, when it was put in force, when it is next due for replacement, which holders can currently use it, whether a replacement or destruction is in progress, and the outcome of the most recent operation over it.

The enumeration SHALL be the basis on which "what still uses the old material" is answered, and SHALL be consistent across instances rather than reflecting only the instance that answered. It SHALL contain no material.

Where the enumeration cannot be produced completely, it SHALL state which part is missing and why, rather than returning a partial answer that reads as complete.

#### Scenario: Enumeration answers what still uses the outgoing material

- **WHEN** a replacement is in progress and an operator enumerates custody state
- **THEN** every item still on the outgoing material is listed with its holders
- **AND** the listing matches the forced-replacement report

#### Scenario: Enumeration states the schedule per class

- **WHEN** an operator enumerates custody state
- **THEN** each item states when its material was put in force and when it is next due for replacement
- **AND** no entry contains material

#### Scenario: Enumeration is consistent across instances

- **WHEN** the enumeration is requested from different instances
- **THEN** each reports the same items, versions, and holders
- **AND** no answer reflects only the answering instance's local state

#### Scenario: An incomplete enumeration says so

- **WHEN** the enumeration cannot be produced completely
- **THEN** the response states which part is missing and why
- **AND** it is not returned as a complete answer

#### Scenario: Enumeration is scoped for a tenant

- **WHEN** a tenant enumerates custody state
- **THEN** only their own items appear, without holder detail that reveals the installation's topology
- **AND** no material appears

### Requirement: Custody work is bounded and yields to serving

`tenancy/isolation` owns the enumeration of per-tenant bound dimensions, and `routing/custom-domains` owns the bounding of claims and certificate issuance per tenant and in aggregate. This capability SHALL enforce those bounds at its own admission point and SHALL NOT introduce a parallel per-tenant dimension of its own.

The background work custody itself creates — generating material, protecting it, re-protecting records across a replacement, verifying destruction, and destroying material — SHALL be bounded in the work it performs before yielding, SHALL NOT block the recovery or use of material on the serving path, and SHALL NOT delay another tenant's serving or another tenant's scheduled replacement beyond the interference tolerance the installation declares.

An operator-forced replacement SHALL take precedence over that background work and over any tenant's generation, and work preempted by it SHALL be named in an operator-visible condition rather than dropped silently.

#### Scenario: Custody work does not block the serving path

- **WHEN** re-protection, destruction verification, and generation all run against a large number of items
- **THEN** recovery and use of material for established and new connections continue to complete throughout
- **AND** the work performed before yielding is bounded

#### Scenario: One tenant's custody work does not delay another's

- **WHEN** one tenant causes custody work continuously at the bound the isolation capability sets for it
- **THEN** another tenant's connections are terminated within the declared interference tolerance
- **AND** another tenant's scheduled replacement is not delayed beyond its bound

#### Scenario: Custody introduces no bound of its own

- **WHEN** a tenant is refused or deferred for custody work
- **THEN** the outcome names a bound the isolation or custom-domain capability defines
- **AND** no bound dimension is named that those capabilities do not enumerate

#### Scenario: A forced replacement preempts background custody work

- **WHEN** an operator forces a replacement while background custody work is saturating its bound
- **THEN** the forced replacement proceeds
- **AND** the preempted work is named in an operator-visible condition rather than dropped silently
